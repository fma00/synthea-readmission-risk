## Why

Slice 2's label counts *any* inpatient→inpatient stay within 30 days as a readmission. CMS HRRP and LACE both exclude **planned** readmissions; slice 2 knowingly did not (named follow-up in `CLAUDE.md` and the slice-2 design doc). Measured consequence in the current gold table (13,222 rows, 1,607 positives = 12.15%):

- Two lung-cancer admission reasons (SNOMED `424132000`, `67811000119102`; 3,289 gold rows) account for 1,166 of 1,607 positives (72.6%). Their readmitting stays are **chemotherapy cycles**: 3,315 of the 3,321 lung-reason inpatient stays carry the procedure `703423002` "Combined chemotherapy and radiation therapy" plus cisplatin/paclitaxel/etoposide (all 3,289 lung stays in the gold table are coded; of the 32 lung stays outside it, 26 are coded and 6 are not — none of the 6 reached the gold table). That procedure is on the readmitting stay of 1,157 of those 1,166 positives and of 30 further non-lung positives (1,187 in total; chemo-*medication* evidence agrees with the chemo-*procedure* evidence on every one of them: 0 disagreements).
- The cardiac "diagnostic → procedure" pattern (158 positives from "Abnormal findings diagnostic imaging heart+coronary circulation", 69 from aortic valve stenosis, 26 from aortic valve regurgitation, …) is **not** a planned readmission; it is one care episode Synthea records as several encounters. The "abnormal imaging"/"aortic stenosis" stay *is* the surgery admission (median sternotomy, intubation, TAVI…), and the "History of CABG / aortic valve replacement" stays are the post-operative ICU/recovery stays, which frequently **overlap in time** with the surgery stay.
- **Stage counts** (pre-2b 13,222-row cohort → the final design): 1,607 positives all-cause → **423** after ignoring planned (chemo) readmissions (1,607 − 1,187 = 420 have no chemo readmit; 3 more have both a chemo and a non-chemo readmit and stay positive) → **218** after also ignoring stays that start at or before the end of an earlier stay of the same patient (the continuation rule below; 205 of the 423 had *only* continuation stays as their non-planned in-window readmissions; 13,214 rows after re-applying the death/censoring rule) → **207** (9,921 rows) after excluding planned index stays → **151** (**9,284 rows**) after keeping only episode-terminal index stays (the terminal rule below).
- Consequence: a reason-code-only lookup reaches test ROC-AUC 0.933 / Brier 0.0670 and neither trained model beats it on AUC (README "Results and caveats"). Slice 4's Top-N list would rank patients mainly by "are you in a scripted lung-cancer cycle".

A second, independent gap: the gold table stores **no provenance**. `TrainingConfig.reference_date` and `readmission_window_days` "MUST equal" the Big Join's, but nothing checks it; a too-late `--reference-date` is undetectable (only a too-early one trips the >1% censoring-buffer warning). The label-semantics change in this slice makes that gap worse: an OLD all-cause gold table has a byte-identical 23-column schema, so it would pass slice 3's strict schema check and train silently under the new label's name.

## Decisions already made with the user (2026-09-20, `AskUserQuestion`)

1. **Label rule:** "chemo/radiation procedure evidence + overlap-continuation rule" (defined exactly below), with exactly the three SNOMED codes shown to the user. Rejected: same-`REASONCODE` rule (removes 127 non-lung non-chemo positives — CABG-history 79, CHF 27, COVID 11 — none with chemo evidence, i.e. plausibly genuine readmissions); lung reason-code allowlist (brittle, Synthea-specific, leaves 452 positives incl. 29 chemo readmissions it cannot see); keep-all-cause-plus-second-column (two labels cannot share one death/censoring cohort without biasing the all-cause column near `reference_date`).
2. **Planned index stays are excluded from the cohort** (3,293 of the 13,222 pre-2b gold rows = 24.9%: 3,289 lung, 3 AML, 1 breast). This goes slightly beyond the literal request (readmission side only) and is flagged as such: keeping them as label 0 leaves a quarter of the table as trivially-zero scripted chemo cycles that inflate AUC (prototype, measured on the 13,214-row cohort with them and the 9,921-row cohort without them — both *before* the terminal rule of decision 5 — lookup AUC 0.862 vs 0.849; LR 0.868 vs 0.846).
3. **Label column name is kept** (`is_readmitted`; semantics change to "unplanned"). The 23-column gold schema and all schema-classification tests are unchanged. The guard against an old all-cause table is the metadata file (decision 4).
4. **Absent metadata ⇒ slice 3 refuses**, with a clear rebuild instruction and no escape-hatch flag. Consequence: the existing `data/gold/full_run` (pre-2b, no metadata) stops being usable by slice 3; it is stale by design.
5. **Only episode-terminal stays are index rows** (approved after the round-3 design critic found the issue). Overlapping stays are treated as one care episode by the continuation rule, so a stay that another stay overlaps past its end is not a discharge: keeping it would (a) duplicate readmission events — the 207 positive rows were only **151 distinct readmission events** (637 non-terminal rows held 56 positives), (b) include rows slice 4 would never score. The cost is stated up front: the test window falls to **41 positives, below slice 3's `MIN_TEST_POSITIVES_FOR_RELIABLE_METRICS = 50`**, so the "metrics and intervals are unreliable" `UserWarning` **will fire on the real run; this is expected and documented, and the threshold is not lowered to silence it.**

### `src/readmission_risk/pipeline/gold_metadata.py` (stdlib only — must NOT import pyspark)

```python
GOLD_METADATA_FILENAME = "_gold_metadata.json"
GOLD_METADATA_VERSION = 1
LABEL_DEFINITION = "unplanned_readmission_v1"
PLANNED_PROCEDURE_CODES: tuple[str, ...] = ("33195004", "367336001", "703423002")

# chemo/radiation codes present (703423002 on 3,315 stays, 367336001 on 4, 33195004 on 1). Strictly sorted, unique.
# Deliberately NOT included: transplant / bone-marrow codes (70536003, 88039007, 58776007, 234336002, 58390007). Measured

@dataclass(frozen=True)

    n_planned_stays: int                     # 0 <= . <= n_inpatient_stays   inpatient stays matching PLANNED_PROCEDURE_CODES
    n_continuation_stays: int                # 0 <= . <= n_inpatient_stays   inpatient stays flagged is_continuation
    n_nonterminal_stays: int                 # 0 <= . <= n_inpatient_stays   inpatient stays flagged NOT is_terminal

    # data-dependent; the two counts are equal on the real data, 682 = 682, but not in general).
```

**`parse_yyyymmdd(value, field)`**: `ValueError(f"{field} must be a YYYYMMDD string; got {value!r}")` unless `value` is a `str`, `value.isascii()`, `value.isdigit()` and `len(value) == 8`; then `ValueError(f"{field} is not a valid calendar date: {value!r}")` if `date(...)` rejects it; returns the `date`. These are the **exact messages of the existing `training._parse_yyyymmdd`** (which is deleted in favour of this one; the only behavioural change is that non-ASCII digits, e.g. full-width `２０２６０９１６`, are now rejected — `isdigit()` alone accepts them).

**Validation lives in one module-level function `_validate(metadata: GoldMetadata, source: str) -> None`** (raising `ValueError`), called by both `write_gold_metadata` and `read_gold_metadata`. `GoldMetadata` itself does **not** validate in `__post_init__` (the tests must be able to construct invalid objects and hand them to `write_gold_metadata`). `planned_procedure_codes` must be a `tuple` at validation time (a `list` is rejected, not coerced; `read_gold_metadata` converts a JSON *array* to a tuple before calling `_validate`; any other JSON type — e.g. the string `"703423002"` — is passed through unchanged and rejected there, never blindly `tuple()`-ed into characters). Validation rules, all raising `ValueError`: `metadata_version == 1`; **every** integer field (including `metadata_version`) is `type(x) is int` — `bool` and `float` are rejected; `label_definition` is a non-empty `str`; `reference_date` passes `parse_yyyymmdd`; every range and the cross-field invariant in the dataclass comments; `planned_procedure_codes` is a non-empty tuple of non-empty `str` that is strictly sorted and duplicate-free. The cross-field invariant's message names **all three** fields (`n_rows`, `n_planned_stays`, `n_inpatient_stays`). `source` (the metadata file path, or `"<in-memory>"` when writing) prefixes every message as `f"{source}: ..."`, and the offending key/field name appears in it; for `reference_date` the reader/writer wrap the `parse_yyyymmdd` `ValueError` so the message still carries `source`.

**File format:** a JSON object with exactly the 12 dataclass field names as keys (`planned_procedure_codes` a JSON array of strings), written as `json.dumps(obj, indent=2, sort_keys=True) + "\n"`, UTF-8.

**`write_gold_metadata`:** runs `_validate` first (an invalid object is never written); does **not** sort or repair anything (unsorted/duplicate codes are rejected, not fixed); raises `FileNotFoundError` if `gold_dir` is not an existing directory; writes `gold_dir/_gold_metadata.json.tmp` then `os.replace`s it onto `_gold_metadata.json` (atomic on POSIX); returns the final path.

**`read_gold_metadata`:**
- file absent (`FileNotFoundError`) → re-raised with the message `Gold metadata not found: <path> -- this gold table predates slice 2b or is incomplete; rebuild it with scripts/run_big_join.py`.
- any other `OSError` while reading (a directory named `_gold_metadata.json`, permissions) → `ValueError` naming the path (so `scripts/train_models.py`'s `(FileNotFoundError, ValueError)` handler prints `ERROR:` instead of a traceback).
- The file is read as bytes and decoded with `utf-8`; a `UnicodeDecodeError` is re-raised as `ValueError` naming the path. JSON is parsed with an `object_pairs_hook` that raises `ValueError` (naming the path and the key) on a **duplicate key** (plain `json.loads` silently keeps the last one, which would make "exactly these 12 keys" unverifiable).
- `ValueError` (message prefixed with the path, plus the key where one exists) for: unparseable JSON; top level not an object; a missing key; an **unknown** key (strict, mirroring the strict gold-schema philosophy); and every `_validate` failure.
- It does **not** compare `label_definition`/`planned_procedure_codes` to the module constants (that is the consumer's decision, so the reader stays usable by tooling that understands other definitions).

### `src/readmission_risk/pipeline/big_join.py`

`PLANNED_PROCEDURE_CODES` and `LABEL_DEFINITION` are imported from `gold_metadata` (single definition); `from pyspark.sql import DataFrame, SparkSession, Window` (the existing import line gains `Window`).

**Definitions** (all over the encounters table; "inpatient stay" = a row with `ENCOUNTERCLASS = 'inpatient'`; non-inpatient encounters take no part in any rule below; `W = readmission_window_days`):

- **planned stay**: an inpatient stay whose `Id` equals the `ENCOUNTER` of at least one `procedures` row whose `CODE` (string equality) is in `PLANNED_PROCEDURE_CODES`. Procedure rows with a null `ENCOUNTER` or `CODE` never match.
- **continuation stay**: an inpatient stay `s` for which, ordering that patient's inpatient stays by `(START ascending, Id ascending)` and looking only at stays strictly *before* `s` in that order, `max(STOP)` over them is non-null and `s.START <= max(STOP)` (the maximum over **all** earlier stays, not just the immediate predecessor). Pinned consequences: a stay that starts exactly when an earlier stay ends (`START == max earlier STOP`) **is** a continuation (6 such stays in the data); one that starts one second later is **not**; of two stays with identical `START` the smaller `Id` is ordered first and is *not* a continuation of the other, while the larger `Id` **is** provided the smaller-`Id` stay has a non-null `STOP` (one tied pair — 2 stays, one of which is flagged — in the data), so the result is deterministic. A stay with null `STOP` contributes nothing to any `max` (Spark's `max` ignores nulls), so a null-`STOP` earlier stay never makes a later stay a continuation — pinned by a test as *current behaviour*, not endorsed as ideal; no null-`STOP` inpatient stay exists in the current export (0 of 13,565). `START` is assumed non-null (a Synthea invariant, not enforced — same posture as slice 2). Continuation is computed over **all** inpatient stays *including planned ones and stays that are not index candidates*, and **before** planned stays are removed anywhere: a stay that overlaps an earlier planned stay is a continuation.
- **terminal stay**: an inpatient stay `c` such that there is **no other** inpatient stay `s` of the same patient (`s.Id != c.Id`) with `s.START <= c.STOP` **and** `s.STOP > c.STOP` (inclusive on the first comparison: a stay that starts exactly when `c` ends and runs on makes `c` non-terminal). Both comparisons are null-false: a stay with null `STOP` never makes another stay non-terminal, and a stay whose own `STOP` is null is trivially terminal (it is never an index candidate anyway). Considered over **all** inpatient stays including planned ones (a planned stay still running at `c`'s stop means `c` is not the discharge that ends the episode). Pinned consequences: `s.STOP == c.STOP` does **not** make `c` non-terminal; `s.START = c.STOP + 1s` does not either; a stay that *contains* `c` does (`s.START <= c.STOP < s.STOP`). 682 non-terminal inpatient stays in the data (equal to the continuation count there — a coincidence, not an identity).
- **index candidate**: an inpatient stay with non-null `STOP` that is **not planned** and **is terminal**. (Planned and non-terminal stays are excluded from the output entirely — not labeled 0.)
- **counting stay** (may be a readmission): an inpatient stay that is neither planned nor a continuation.
- **Label**: `is_readmitted = 1` iff there exists a counting stay `a` with the same `PATIENT`, `a.Id != c.Id`, `a.START > c.STOP` (strict) and `a.START <= c.STOP + INTERVAL W DAYS` (**inclusive** upper bound) for index candidate `c`; otherwise 0. The window is `(stop, stop+W]`, unchanged from slice 2. (The strict lower bound is redundant with the continuation/terminal rules on any non-degenerate data — inpatient stays in the export last ≥ 1 day — and is kept unchanged from slice 2; a zero-length-stay test pins it.)
- **Death / censoring exclusion — rule unchanged, but now evaluated against the NEW label**: keep a candidate iff `is_readmitted = 1` OR ((`DEATHDATE` is null OR `DEATHDATE > readmit_deadline`) AND `readmit_deadline < reference_date + INTERVAL 1 DAY`), with `readmit_deadline = STOP + W days`. Because the label is computed first and the keep-rule second, a former "positive despite censoring/death" whose only readmissions were planned/continuations becomes a censored/deceased negative and is **dropped** (measured: 8 rows).

**New public helper** (the single place the three flags are computed; used by `build_index_encounters` and by `build_big_join`'s diagnostics):

```python
def flag_inpatient_stays(encounters: DataFrame, procedures: DataFrame) -> DataFrame:
    """All inpatient stays with four added columns: prev_max_stop (timestamp, nullable), is_continuation, is_planned and
    is_terminal (all boolean, non-null). Extra input columns are preserved. One row per inpatient stay (no fan-out)."""
    inpatient = encounters.filter(F.col("ENCOUNTERCLASS") == "inpatient")
    w = Window.partitionBy("PATIENT").orderBy("START", "Id").rowsBetween(Window.unboundedPreceding, -1)
    planned_ids = (
        procedures.filter(F.col("CODE").isin(*PLANNED_PROCEDURE_CODES))
        .select(F.col("ENCOUNTER").alias("_planned_encounter_id"))
        .distinct()                                    # required: a stay has many planned procedure rows (19,666 rows / 3,320 stays)
        .withColumn("_planned_marker", F.lit(True))
    )
    x, y = inpatient.alias("x"), inpatient.alias("y")
    nonterminal_ids = (
        x.join(
            y,
            (F.col("x.PATIENT") == F.col("y.PATIENT")) & (F.col("x.Id") != F.col("y.Id"))
            & (F.col("y.START") <= F.col("x.STOP")) & (F.col("y.STOP") > F.col("x.STOP")),
            "left_semi",
        )
        .select(F.col("x.Id").alias("_nonterminal_id"))
        .distinct()
        .withColumn("_nonterminal_marker", F.lit(True))
    )
    return (
        inpatient.withColumn("prev_max_stop", F.max("STOP").over(w))
        .withColumn("is_continuation", F.col("prev_max_stop").isNotNull() & (F.col("START") <= F.col("prev_max_stop")))
        .join(planned_ids, F.col("Id") == F.col("_planned_encounter_id"), "left")
        .withColumn("is_planned", F.coalesce(F.col("_planned_marker"), F.lit(False)))
        .join(nonterminal_ids, F.col("Id") == F.col("_nonterminal_id"), "left")
        .withColumn("is_terminal", ~F.coalesce(F.col("_nonterminal_marker"), F.lit(False)))
        .drop("_planned_encounter_id", "_planned_marker", "_nonterminal_id", "_nonterminal_marker")
    )
```

**`build_index_encounters`** (the new third parameter is inserted positionally; every existing call site is updated):

```python
def build_index_encounters(
    encounters: DataFrame,
    patients: DataFrame,
    procedures: DataFrame,          # needs columns ENCOUNTER, CODE (both nullable strings)
    reference_date: date,
    readmission_window_days: int = 30,
) -> DataFrame:
    flagged = flag_inpatient_stays(encounters, procedures)
    candidates = flagged.filter(
        F.col("STOP").isNotNull() & ~F.col("is_planned") & F.col("is_terminal")
    ).withColumn("readmit_deadline", F.expr(f"STOP + INTERVAL {readmission_window_days} DAYS"))
    readmit_pool = flagged.filter(~F.col("is_planned") & ~F.col("is_continuation"))
    c, a = candidates.alias("c"), readmit_pool.alias("a")
    # ... the remainder (left_semi on (c.PATIENT == a.PATIENT) & (a.Id != c.Id) & (a.START > c.STOP)
    #     & (a.START <= c.readmit_deadline); left-join back + F.when(...).otherwise(0); DEATHDATE join;
    #     keep_condition; the final .select) is EXACTLY slice 2's existing code, unchanged.
```

## Data handling

- **Feature leakage — unchanged and re-checked.** No feature column changes. The label rule reads only encounters/procedures/patients and is computed independent of the feature columns; features stay `[window_start, index_start)`. `procedure_count_window` may count chemotherapy procedures from *earlier* cycles of a retained (non-planned) index patient — legitimate pre-index history.
- **New label-side property (flagged, measured, not fixed).** Slice 2's label depended only on a readmit's `START`; the new label also depends on the readmitting stay's *content* (its procedures) and on earlier stays' `STOP`s. Slice 3's purge (`index_stop + W < test_start`) guarantees a readmit's *start* precedes the test period but not that everything that classifies it does. **Precise measure** (used by the manual step): for each row of `split.train`, take every inpatient stay `a` of the same patient — planned, continuation or counting alike — with `index_stop < a.START <= index_stop + W`; `horizon(a) = max(a.STOP, max STOP over all that patient's inpatient stays ordered before a by (START, Id))`, ignoring null `STOP`s; the property holds iff `max horizon(a)` over all (row, a) pairs is `< test_start` (2023-01-01T00:00Z). Measured on the real data with the final cohort: **118** of the 6,391 train rows have at least one such in-window stay `a`, and the property **holds — 0 of those 118 rows** have a `horizon(a) >= test_start`; the maximum horizon is **2022-12-25T01:59:59Z**, only about 6 days before `test_start`, so the margin is thin. **A second, related property** (cohort membership, not label): a train row is dropped as non-terminal if some overlapping stay `y` has `y.STOP > c.STOP`, and `y.STOP` may fall at or after `test_start` (the longest stay in the data is 80 days, longer than the 30-day purge). Measured: of 470 non-terminal, non-planned candidates whose `STOP + 30d < test_start`, **0** are dropped because of a `y` ending on or after `test_start`. Both are properties of this dataset, not guarantees; tightening the purge is a named follow-up.
- **Split integrity — unchanged.** The chronological + patient-disjoint split, censoring buffer and label-window purge logic in `split.py` are untouched (only its docstring was corrected). The label window is still `(stop, stop+W]` inclusive, so the purge/buffer arithmetic stays valid. The death/censoring rule is applied after labeling; the 8 rows it newly drops, the planned-index exclusion and the non-terminal exclusion are the only row-set changes. Final-cohort split summary at `test_start=2023-01-01`: `n_input` 9,284, `n_dropped_censor_buffer` 1, `n_test` 1,539 (41 positive), `n_dropped_label_window_purge` 34, `n_dropped_patient_overlap` 1,319, `n_train` 6,391 (96 positive), 4,167 train / 1,295 test patients.
- **Population change (intended).** The gold cohort is now "non-planned inpatient discharges that end an episode". Slice 4 must score only that population. Caveat for slice 4's design: the gold table contains only *labelled historical* rows (it drops discharges whose 30-day window is still open), so scoring **new** discharges requires applying the planned/terminal filter to unlabelled stays — `flag_inpatient_stays` is the starting point for that filter but is **not directly reusable as-is**: its null-`STOP` semantics (an open stay never makes another stay non-terminal or a continuation) are wrong for scoring, where a still-open overlapping stay is exactly the case that matters (a named follow-up, not built here). Features remain **stay-level** (lookback windows, length of stay and age are anchored on the terminal stay, not the episode's first stay) — episode-level features are a named follow-up.
- **Residual scripted patterns — the motivating problem is reduced, not gone (measured, standing-rule flag).** After all rules the positive rate falls from 12.15% to 1.63% (151 / 9,284) but the label is still reason-concentrated. Positives by admission reason, counts only: **all 151** — CABG-history 45, CHF 30, aortic stenosis 20, aortic regurgitation 17, dependent drug abuse 7, no recorded reason 7, NSTEMI 7, abnormal cardiac imaging 5, … (the five largest reasons hold 119 of 151); **train 96** — CHF 20, aortic stenosis 18, CABG-history 17, aortic regurgitation 15, then four reasons at 4 each; **test 41** — **CABG-history 23 (56%)**, CHF 10, three reasons at 2 each (dependent drug abuse, NSTEMI, no recorded reason) and two singletons. The 45 CABG-history positives are same-reason repeats for 43 of them (median gap over all 45: 16.6 days; over the 43: 16.7 days) — they carry a generic four-procedure post-operative bundle but no planned-procedure code, so the rule cannot classify them as planned (see "Label v2" below). A reason-code lookup will therefore very likely remain strong. This is reported, not fixed, in this slice (see Acceptance above and the follow-ups).
- **Known residual of the terminal rule.** It removes mid-episode index rows but two stays with identical `STOP` (or identical `START` and `STOP`) are both terminal and both remain index rows — the very duplication decision 5 argues against. There are **0** such same-patient identical-`STOP` pairs in the real data, and the final 151 positive rows have 151 distinct first-readmit stays (first = earliest `START`), so no duplicated event remains on this data; the rule is pinned by the `A3`/`B3` fixture and is a documented limitation for other data.
- **Small-sample and prevalence-shift warning (the reliability warning WILL fire).** The test window holds **41 positives, below `MIN_TEST_POSITIVES_FOR_RELIABLE_METRICS = 50`**, so `train_and_evaluate` emits its `UserWarning` ("… metrics and intervals are unreliable"); this is expected and documented, and the threshold is not changed. With 96 train positives and 5 patient-grouped calibration folds (positives per calibration fold on the real data: 15, 25, 13, 21, 22) expect very wide intervals. The positive rate also shifts between partitions (**1.50% train vs 2.66% test**), which directly affects calibration/ECE and slice 3's Brier skill score (which uses the *train* prevalence as its reference); the results write-up must state this shift rather than let a miscalibration read as a model defect. The test window (≥ 2023-01-01) is **still a development set, now for the third design decision made after looking at it** (cutoff, feature exclusions, label rule); the README's "not a virgin holdout" language is retained.
- **Reproducibility.** The Big Join is a deterministic function of its CSV inputs (no randomness); the window's `(START, Id)` tie-break removes the only order-dependence this slice adds. Slice 3's seeds are unchanged (`seed=42`). `gold_fingerprint` changes (different rows/labels) and is re-recorded.
- **Recalled-not-verified claims.** That CMS's Planned Readmission Algorithm treats chemotherapy/radiotherapy/transplant as "always planned" and does not count transfers/overlapping stays as readmissions, and that CMS's hospital-wide measure excludes cancer-treatment index admissions, is from memory, not verified this session. The docs will say "as recalled; verify before citing".

## Failure modes

| Situation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `_gold_metadata.json` absent (old gold, partial copy, failed build) | `FileNotFoundError` with rebuild instruction; `train_models.py` prints `ERROR: …`, exit 1 |
| Metadata unreadable (`OSError`, bad UTF-8), malformed, duplicate/unknown key, bad type/value, `metadata_version != 1` | `ValueError` naming path (+ key); exit 1 |
| `label_definition` or `planned_procedure_codes` ≠ this code's constants | `ValueError` naming both sides; exit 1 |
| Parquet rows or positives ≠ metadata `n_rows` / `n_positive` | `ValueError` (incomplete copy / not the described table) |
| `TrainingConfig.reference_date` or `readmission_window_days` ≠ metadata | `ValueError` before any split/MLflow side effect (closes the too-late-`reference_date` gap) |
| `procedures.csv` present but empty | `load_synthea_tables` → `ValueError` (before any Spark read of it) |
| `procedures.csv` non-empty but no code ever matches (Synthea code change, wrong `SYSTEM`) | build succeeds; `UserWarning`; metadata `n_planned_stays = 0`; CLI prints it; the manual step asserts the exact count |
| `BigJoinConfig` window/lookback not a true `int` ≥ 1, malformed reference date | `ValueError` before any I/O |
| Metadata write fails after Parquet write | directory left without metadata → slice 3 refuses it; delete and re-run |
| No planned stays in a small input | label = "continuation/terminal rules only"; legal, warned |
| No inpatient stays at all (e.g. only ambulatory encounters) | zero-row gold + metadata with all counts 0; no warning; slice 3 then raises its existing zero-row `ValueError` |
| Every candidate excluded | same zero-row behaviour (slice 2's tolerance retained) |
| Two inpatient stays with identical `(PATIENT, START)` | deterministic via `Id` tie-break |
| Fewer than 50 test positives | slice 3's existing `UserWarning` (fires on the real run, expected) |
| MLflow experiment already holds runs whose `gold_label_definition` tag is missing or differs (e.g. pre-2b runs in the default `readmission-risk` experiment) | `UserWarning` naming the count, before any run is logged; never a refusal (added in the pre-commit review) |

## Label v2 — what the remaining positives are, and four candidate definitions

*Added after the slice was committed (2026-09-20), in response to the question of why the models still cannot beat a reason-code lookup. Every figure is reproducible from the measurements note ("Label residuals and label-v2 candidates"). Descriptive analysis on the development data; not a validated taxonomy.*

### Findings: the 151 remaining positives fall into groups that behave very differently

| Group | Positives | What the data shows |
|---|---|---|
| **CHF** | 30 | Deterioration-like: **27 of 30 readmitting stays begin through the emergency department**, same reason 27, median gap 12.0 days, readmitting stays vary in length (median 61 h; index stays median 121 h). |
| **CABG-history** | 45 | Scripted: **all 401** CABG-history index stays last exactly 24 h, and 391 carry the identical four-procedure bundle (discharge from hospital, postoperative education, stair-climbing test, walking exercise test). 44 of the 45 readmitting stays are the same fixed 24-h stay (43 same reason); **1 of 45 begins via the ED**; gap median 16.6 d (0.7–28.7). | **Aortic valve** (stenosis 20, regurgitation 17) | 37 | 30 of the 37 readmitting stays are the post-operative "History of aortic valve replacement" stay; the gap is short (median 30 h, range 3 h to 27 days; 25 of 37 within 72 h); **0 begin via the ED**. |
| **Scattered** (NSTEMI 7, drug abuse 7, no recorded reason 7, abnormal imaging 5, transplant 3, …) | 39 | Mixed, small counts. |

**Correction to earlier wording.** The design note, README and CLAUDE.md previously said the CABG-history recurrences carry "no procedure evidence". That was imprecise: they *do* carry procedures (the generic bundle above), just none in `PLANNED_PROCEDURE_CODES`. That is exactly why the planned-procedure rule cannot remove them.

**The separating marker is emergency-department initiation.** Only 4.7% of all inpatient stays begin through the ED (same-instant emergency encounter; 8.1% within 1 h, 8.3% within 24 h). Of the 151 positives, **30 have an ED-initiated counting readmission: CHF 27, CABG-history 1, kidney-transplant 1, bone-marrow-transplant 1; every other reason has 0**. Real-world claims definitions commonly treat an emergency/urgent admission as the marker of an *unplanned* admission (as recalled; verify before citing), so this is a standard-aligned signal, not an ad hoc one. The other two markers seen in the data are the fixed 24-h duration and the status-post ("history of …", a SNOMED *situation* concept rather than an admitting diagnosis) reason codes; both are Synthea-specific.

### Four candidate definitions, each measured (relabel → re-apply death/censoring → chronological split at 2023-01-01)

| # | Definition | Rows | Positives | Train (pos) | Test (pos) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — | **Current label** (slice 2b) | 9,284 | 151 (1.63%) | 6,391 (96) | 1,539 (41) |
| 1 | **ED-initiated readmission**: a readmitting stay counts only if it begins via an ED encounter | 9,282 | 30 (0.32%) | 6,390 (21) | 1,539 (**9**) |
| 2a | **Exclude status-post reasons as index rows** (CABG-history `399261000`, AVR-history `1231000119100`) | 8,791 | 106 (1.21%) | 6,188 (80) | 1,381 (**18**) |
| 2b | … and also as readmitting stays | 8,791 | 65 (0.74%) | 6,188 (43) | 1,381 (**16**) |
| 3a | **HRRP-style condition cohorts**: index restricted to HF + AMI (CHF `88805009`, NSTEMI `401314000`) | 610 | 37 (6.07%) | 506 (25) | 97 (**12**) |
| 3b | … heart failure only | 341 | 30 (8.80%) | 290 (20) | 44 (**10**) |
| 4a | **Acute-admission cohort**: the index stay must itself begin via the ED | 457 | 31 (6.78%) | 362 (21) | 88 (**10**) |
| 4b | … and the readmitting stay must too | 457 | 27 (5.91%) | 362 (18) | 88 (**9**) |

1. **ED-initiated readmission.** One rule, no code list, and it generalises to other Synthea modules. The label becomes almost entirely CHF exacerbations. Depends on the Synthea convention that an emergency encounter and its inpatient stay share a start instant (an exact-match test; a 1-hour window changes the share of ED-initiated stays from 4.7% to 8.1%, and was not re-run at positive level). Would miss unplanned admissions that do not pass through the ED (none observed here). Needs `begins_via_ed` added to `flag_inpatient_stays`.
2. **Status-post reasons.** Cheap and transparent, but a Synthea-specific code list, the brittleness the chemo evidence rule was chosen to avoid. 2a alone leaves the 37 aortic-valve positives (whose readmitting stays are the "History of aortic valve replacement" stays); 2b is needed to remove them.
3. **HRRP-style condition cohorts** (as recalled, CMS HRRP defines readmission cohorts by condition rather than all-cause; verify before citing). The standard structure, and the positive rate looks high (6–9%) only because the cohort is small and CHF-heavy; it is not comparable to the other rates. The choice of cohorts is arbitrary within Synthea (pneumonia and COPD are not among the top reasons here).
4. **Acute-admission cohort.** Defines the population as discharges after an emergency admission, which is conceptually the cleanest match to a discharge-triage purpose. Only 457 index rows, and slice 4 would then score only ED-initiated discharges.

**Recommendation.** At the current 10,000-patient size no stricter definition can be evaluated: the test window would hold 9–18 positives (the current label already falls below the 50-positive reliability threshold at 41). Adoption should therefore wait for the larger population, where option 1 and option 4b project (arithmetic, not a measurement) to roughly 300–600 positives, about 90–180 of them in the test window. Any adoption is a new label identity: bump `LABEL_DEFINITION` to `unplanned_readmission_v2`, update the change-detector test, the metadata, and the docs; the metadata guard then refuses every gold table built under v1. Slice 4 should be designed so that its scoring-side eligibility filter is parameterised by the label definition rather than hard-coding v1.
